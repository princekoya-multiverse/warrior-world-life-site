Our Story

It started with a car accident

The New Westminster Record: "Beloved New West wrestler raising money for his wife after horrific crash."

January 2021. A morning of hope. A moment that split life into before and after.

Prince (Rish Koya) and Chelsy were driving through Osoyoos when a catastrophic crash on Anarchist Mountain changed everything. The jaws of life had to be used to extract Chelsy.

Her injuries were devastating: a fractured spine, multiple fractures in the pelvis, a broken dominant left arm and wrist, plus 10 staples to the head. She spent 35 days across Kelowna, Penticton, and Oliver hospitals.

Prince suffered a concussion, with injuries to his neck and right hand. But as the New Westminster Record reported: "Chelsy took the worst of it and her injuries are far more serious."

The doctors said 98% of patients her age end up hooked on opiates. They said she'd never walk normally again. Prince refused to accept that prognosis.

He brought the Prime Medical Device into the hospital room. PEMF therapy. Plant medicine. Alkalized water. Bolstered protocols.

She was off all narcotics in five days.

A GoFundMe campaign raised money for her recovery, and the City of New Westminster rallied — over $10,000 raised. Chelsy attacked her recovery the same way she attacks her athletic training — "with ruthless aggression".

This is not a testimonial. This is clinical proof on camera.

"If the system can't save the person you love most, you build your own system. So I did."

— Prince Koya, Founder
The Turning Point

He helped Chelsy heal. Then he had to save himself.

For all intents and purposes, we did miracles with Chelsy. She still lives with invisible disabilities — they don't disappear — but she walks, she trains, she attacks life with ruthless aggression.

But here's the part we don't hide: while Prince was helping Chelsy, he was not taking care of himself.

Years of concussions from professional wrestling. Degenerative Disc Disease at C6-C7. Nerve damage down his entire right side. And the stress of the crash, the hospitals, the fundraising — everything compounded.

By 2023, he had eight invisible disabilities. Not a single one shows up on an x-ray. The medical system said: manage it. Accept the limitations. Never fight again.

He got to a state where he had to get help — and get help fast.

So he did what he's always done: he documented everything. Filmed every session. Tracked every metric. And turned his own body into the laboratory — the same system that helped Chelsy walk again.

That documentation became Warrior Season 1 — the 90-day documentary we're filming right now.

"They said I'd never wrestle again. Watch what happens."

— Prince Koya
